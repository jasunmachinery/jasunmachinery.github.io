The Soap Manufacturing Process From Vegetable Oil / Animal Fats and Caustic Soda

The whole process is divided into two major steps.

The first step (Part A) is saponification and drying system to produce soap noodles. It is the saponification reaction of Vegetable Oil / Animal Fats and caustic soda to form a soap base. This soap base is then processed through a vacuum drying system and a pelletizer, which extrudes them into soap noodles.

The second step (Part B) is from soap noodles to produce finished soap. The soap noodles are put into a soap mixer, which blends the soap noodles with perfume and pigment. The mixture is then fed into a refine plodder (refiner). Its core objective is to thoroughly blend, homogenize, and mill the soap materials. After that, the soap noodles come to a three roll mill (three-roller grinder), where the soap noodles are ground into thin soap sheets. These thin sheets are transferred via a conveyor belt to a duplex vacuum soap plodder machine or a single screw soap plodder machine (soap extruder), where they are refined again and continuously extruded into a long soap bar with the desired shape. Afterward, the long bar is cut and shaped into individual soap bars of the required size using an automatic soap cutting machine (soap cutter) or a soap stamping machine (soap stamper / soap printer / soap press / soap punching machine). Finally, the finished soap bars are wrapped by a packaging machine (flow pillow soap packing machine / soap cartoning machine / paper soap packing machine).

For most beginners with no experience in soap manufacturing and a small budget, we suggest choosing Part B for the initial production.

A Complete Set of Soap Production Line From Oil Saponification to Finished Soap

Flow Chart: From Soap Noodles to Finished Soap

  1. Mixer – uniformly blends soap noodles with perfume, pigments, essential oils and additives.
  2. Refiner – pre-refines the mixed soap mass.
  3. Three roll mill – mills the soap for smoothness and uniform color and fragrance.
  4. Duplex plodder (vacuum) – extrudes the refined soap into a continuous, dense bar.
  5. Cutter – cuts the long bar into uniform blanks.
  6. Stamper – stamps blanks into finished bars with logos and shapes.
  7. Packing machine – wraps the finished soap for retail.

Conveyors link each stage; the water chiller and glycol chiller provide process cooling.

Soap Noodles Mixing, Refining and Milling Equipment

3

Industrial Soap Mixer

Core Functions and Working Principle: This equipment is a critical piece of machinery in the front-end stage of the soap production line. It is primarily used to highly efficiently and uniformly blend soap noodles with various additives, such as perfumes, pigments, essential oils, and other auxiliary ingredients. The mixer is equipped with internal screw blades. During operation, the motor drives the main shaft to rotate, utilizing the mechanical motion of the screw to continuously lift, tumble, and scatter the soap noodles from the bottom. This powerful shearing and convective action allows liquid perfumes and solid pigments to rapidly penetrate and evenly coat the soap noodles in a short time. Consequently, it establishes a smooth, refined, and consistently colored soap base foundation for the subsequent milling and plodding (extruding) processes.

Structural Features:
Drive and Power System: Equipped with a high-performance electric motor on the left side, the machine smoothly transmits power to the main mixing shaft via pulleys and a gear reducer, providing high-torque mixing and shearing force.
Mixing Tank/Hopper: The top features a large-capacity, open-top charging hopper, making it easy to feed in soap noodles and auxiliary raw materials.
Discharge Design: A manually controlled discharge gate (outlet) is located at the bottom of the mixing tank. Once mixing is complete, it can be manually opened to discharge the uniformly blended materials into a lower container or onto a conveyor belt.
Materials and Frame: The entire unit is supported by a robust metal steel frame. The parts in contact with the material are typically made of stainless steel to prevent corrosion from fragrances or chemicals and to ensure production hygiene.

4

Refine Plodder

Applications:
Deep Refining and Homogenization: This equipment is primarily used to apply intense mechanical extrusion and shearing to the mixed or milled soap materials. It breaks down the microscopic particles of the soap body further, ensuring that the perfumes, pigments, soap noodles, and auxiliary ingredients are deeply integrated at a molecular level while eliminating internal air bubbles.
Essential for Premium Toilet Soap: It is chiefly utilized in the production of toilet soap and bar soap. Toilet soap demands exceptionally high standards for texture, smoothness, and foam fineness, making this machine indispensable for increasing the density and compactness of the soap base.
A Bridge in the Process Line: Within the production line, it is typically positioned after the three-roll mill and before the soap cutter/stamping machine, taking responsibility for converting loose soap material into a continuous, dense soap bar.

Key Features:
Powerful Screw Extrusion System: The internal core consists of a single or twin-screw with a large lead. Through forceful propulsion and enclosed extrusion, it generates immense mechanical shearing force, which forces the physical modification of the soap materials, rendering them more ductile and resilient.
End Refining Mesh/Plate Design: At the front end of the discharge outlet it is generally equipped with a fine orifice plate and multi-layer stainless steel filter meshes. As the soap material is forced through the filter, any remaining tiny hard particles are thoroughly crushed or intercepted, ensuring the final soap surface is silky smooth with zero graininess.

5

Three Roller Mill

Crystal Transformation and Modification: After mixing, the internal crystallization of the soap may be uneven. The three-roll mill utilizes high-pressure extrusion to force the transformation of soap crystals, forming a crystalline structure with better ductility and resilience. This ensures that the final soap is less prone to cracking and does not easily become mushy when soaked in water.
Complete Elimination of Graininess: The mixed soap material may contain incompletely dissolved solid pigments, additives, or hard soap chunks. Through the ultimate crushing action of the three rollers, these tiny particles are completely pulverized, ensuring that the soap texture is smooth and refined.
Deep Homogenization: Under intense shearing force, perfumes, essential oils, and additives undergo a secondary compounding with the soap base at a microscopic level, rendering the color and fragrance of the entire batch perfectly consistent.

Auxiliary Requirements:

11

Low Temperature Chiller

Precision Deep Mould Cooling: This equipment primarily circulates and delivers low-temperature cooling liquid to provide continuous and forced temperature reduction for the metal moulds inside the automatic soap stamping machine.
Achieving Perfect and Easy Demolding: When ambient or lukewarm soap blanks undergo high-pressure stamping inside the moulds, they are highly susceptible to adhesion caused by friction and compression. By freezing the moulds, the chiller induces a minute thermal shrinkage on the soap surface in contact with the metal, thereby allowing the soap bar to disengage from the mould extremely smoothly and easily.
Defining Finished Visual Quality: Without the deep-cooling protection of a chiller, the soap is highly prone to mould sticking, smudging, or edge damage during the demolding process. The chiller ensures that every finished soap bar leaving the line possesses a mirror-like bright surface and a crisp, clear logo.

12

Cooling Water Machine

Frictional Heat Dissipation to Prevent Soap Melting: As the three-roll mill tears soap at high speeds and the vacuum plodder extrudes soap under heavy duty and high pressure, a substantial amount of physical frictional heat is generated. This equipment injects circulating chilled water into the hollow rollers of the mill and the barrels/jackets of the plodder to forcefully carry away heat, preventing the soap from softening, melting, or sticking to the rollers due to overheating.
Preserving Volatile Fragrances and Heat-Sensitive Components: The perfumes, natural essential oils, and special active ingredients added to soap are mostly heat-sensitive substances that easily volatilize or deteriorate at elevated temperatures. The cooling water machine strictly locks the processing temperature within an acceptable ambient or slightly cool range, perfectly preserving the fragrance and efficacy of the soap.
Ensuring Extrusion Consistency and Structure: During the vacuum extrusion process, if the barrel temperature rises too high, the soap may deform or develop a loose internal structure because of localized surface melting. Through precise cooling water temperature control, it guarantees that the extruded soap bar maintains a highly uniform and compact internal texture.
